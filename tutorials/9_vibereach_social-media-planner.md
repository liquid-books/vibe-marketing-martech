## The Concept: Social Media as a System, Not a Habit

Most people fail at social media because they treat it as a habit ("I should post more"). That framing produces bursts of posting followed by months of silence. The platforms reward consistency, so inconsistent posting performs worse than no posting at all.

The frame that actually works: social media is a *system*. You batch-create content once a week. You schedule it all to publish on its own. You spend zero time on the actual day a post goes live. The system handles distribution; your only job is feeding it well-made content.

The Social Planner is what makes this system possible inside VibeReach without paying for a separate tool like Hootsuite or Buffer.

## Step 1 — Connect Your LinkedIn Account

Navigate to **Marketing → Social Planner** in the left navigation. The first time you open this, the calendar will be empty. Click the **settings gear icon** to start connecting accounts.

1. Click **+ Connect a New LinkedIn Profile** (or LinkedIn Company Page, depending on what you're connecting).
2. A LinkedIn OAuth window opens. Sign into the LinkedIn account that owns the profile or page you want to connect.
3. Grant the requested permissions. LinkedIn will ask for permission to read your profile data and publish posts on your behalf.
4. After authorization, you'll be prompted to select whether you're connecting your personal profile, a Company Page you manage, or both. For most solo consultants, connecting *both* makes sense — your personal profile is where the warmest audience lives (people who know you), while a Company Page is where you can build a brand asset over time.
5. Confirm and save the connection.

The LinkedIn account now appears in your Social Planner's account list. You can connect additional platforms the same way — each follows its own OAuth flow.

### A Few Gotchas

**Instagram requires a Business or Creator account.** Personal Instagram profiles can't be connected to the Social Planner because of Meta API restrictions. If you only have a personal Instagram, convert it to a Creator account first (free, done in the Instagram app settings).

**Facebook Groups require an extra step.** To post to a Facebook Group, you have to add the LeadConnector app to the group itself. In Facebook, go to your group → Settings → Apps → Add App → search for LeadConnector → add it. After that, the group will appear in the Social Planner.

**LinkedIn Company Pages require admin role.** You can only connect Company Pages where your LinkedIn account is listed as an admin. If you're trying to connect a Company Page and don't see it in the list, you don't have admin permissions on that page.

**Periodic re-authorization.** Every 60-90 days, some platforms (especially Facebook and LinkedIn) require you to re-authorize the connection. If posts start failing with auth errors, that's the cause. Go back to Settings → Integrations and reconnect.

## Step 2 — Understand the Composer

Click **+ New Post** in the Social Planner. The composer opens. This is where you'll write, attach media, and schedule.

**Account Selector.** At the top, choose which connected accounts will receive this post. You can publish identical content to multiple platforms at once (Facebook + LinkedIn + Twitter from one composer), or customize the caption per platform if you want different language for different audiences. Toggle off platforms you don't want this post going to.

**Caption Field.** The main text of your post. Character count is shown at the bottom — important because each platform has different limits (Twitter/X at 280, LinkedIn at 3,000, Facebook and Instagram much longer). The composer warns you if you exceed a platform's limit before you schedule.

**Media Attachment.** Click the image or video icon to add media. Single image, multiple images (carousel), video, or none. Image specs vary by platform — the composer auto-optimizes for each platform's recommended dimensions, but you can also customize crops per platform if needed.

**Custom Thumbnails for Video.** If you attach video, you can set a custom thumbnail. Hover over the video preview, click the pencil icon, upload an image. Custom thumbnails are supported on Facebook Pages, Instagram Business, LinkedIn Profiles and Pages, and YouTube. They significantly outperform auto-generated thumbnails — worth the 30 seconds.

**First Comment.** A separate field for an automatic first comment that posts immediately after the main post goes live. This is the standard place for hashtags on Instagram (so they don't clutter the main caption) and for follow-up CTAs or additional links on LinkedIn.

**Schedule Options.** At the bottom: Post Now (publish immediately), Schedule for Later (pick date and time), Save as Draft, Send for Approval (if you have team approval workflows set up), or Schedule as Recurring (post automatically on a repeat schedule).

**AI Caption Generation.** The Content AI integration from Tutorial 7 is here. You can prompt the AI to generate caption variations or rewrite your draft for a specific platform. Same advice as before: use it for first drafts, edit heavily, never publish AI output as-is.

## Step 3 — Plan the Post Before You Write It

Same plan-on-paper discipline as every other tutorial. The reason most social posts fail is that they were written without a plan. Before you open the composer, answer four questions:

**What's the one thing I want the reader to walk away thinking?** Not three things. One. Pick the single insight, observation, or perspective the post is built around.

**Who specifically am I writing to?** Not "small business owners." Specifically: a service business owner with 1-10 employees who's currently doing their CRM work in spreadsheets and is starting to feel the pain. That's a real person. You can write to that person.

**What's the call to action?** A LinkedIn post can have one CTA at most. Examples: "Reply with your biggest CRM frustration and I'll send you a customized fix." Or: "If you want to see how I'd set this up for your business, here's my calendar link." Or: "Hit follow — I'm posting about this every Tuesday for the next month." Pick one.

**What's the hook?** The first 1-2 sentences that determine whether anyone reads the rest. LinkedIn's algorithm shows roughly the first 200 characters before the "see more" cutoff. If your hook isn't compelling by character 200, the post is dead.

Write these four answers on paper. Now you have something to write.

## Step 4 — Write the Post

For this tutorial, let's write a real example. The scenario: you're trying to drive traffic to the CRM consulting funnel from Tutorial 1. Your audience is service business owners. The CTA is the calendar booking link from Tutorial 5.

A draft post:

---

*Most "CRM problems" aren't CRM problems.*

*They're naming problems.*

*Last week I sat with a service business doing $1.4M annual revenue and three years on the same CRM. The owner couldn't tell me how many leads came from referrals vs. paid ads. Couldn't tell me which marketing channel produced their best customers. Couldn't tell me how long deals were sitting in any one pipeline stage.*

*The CRM had all the data. Every contact tagged. Every opportunity logged. Every email tracked.*

*But the tags were named "lead1," "lead2," "interesting." The pipeline stages were "stage 1," "stage 2," "stage 3." The custom fields were labeled "field A" and "field B."*

*The CRM was working perfectly. The owner just couldn't read it.*

*Three weeks of cleanup — renaming tags into actual categories, mapping stages to real moments in the deal cycle, labeling custom fields with what they actually capture — and suddenly the same data told a clear story. Two referral sources doing 60% of revenue. One paid channel that was profitable; one that was losing money. A stage where deals sat for 35 days on average (should have been 5).*

*Same CRM. Same data. New names.*

*If your dashboard tells you nothing, the issue probably isn't your CRM. It's that nobody ever taught the system what to call things.*

*I do this work for $2,500 per engagement. If your CRM has all the data but you can't read it, here's a 20-minute call to walk through what I'd change: [calendar link]*

---

What's working in this post:

- **Hook in the first 200 characters.** "Most 'CRM problems' aren't CRM problems. They're naming problems." That's a counterintuitive opening that earns the click on "see more."
- **Specific story.** A real business, real revenue figure, real time frame. Specificity is what makes social writing land. Vague observations get scrolled past; specific stories get read.
- **A real insight.** The post says something the reader hasn't heard before. Even if they don't book a call, they share it because it made them think.
- **Single clear CTA.** One link, one ask, one offer. No "also follow me, also subscribe, also check out my..."
- **Honest pricing.** Including the $2,500 figure does two things: it pre-qualifies the audience (people who can't afford it self-select out), and it shows the writer takes their work seriously enough to name a price publicly. Both improve close rates from the calls you do get.

What's *not* working:

- It's slightly long for LinkedIn (around 1,200 characters; LinkedIn algorithm slightly favors 1,500-3,000 for engagement but penalizes too-long posts).
- No image attached. Posts with images outperform plain text on LinkedIn by 30-50% in most studies. Should add a simple image — maybe a screenshot of a poorly-named pipeline next to a cleanly-named one.

We'll improve those things, then schedule.

## Step 5 — Add UTM-Tagged Link

The calendar link from Tutorial 5 needs UTM parameters appended so the Attribution Report (from Tutorial 8) tells you exactly which LinkedIn post drove which booked calls.

Take your calendar URL — say it's `https://yourdomain.com/widget/booking/crm-setup` — and append UTM parameters:

```
https://yourdomain.com/widget/booking/crm-setup?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=may-content&utm_content=naming-problems-post
```

Five parts:

- **utm_source=linkedin** — the platform
- **utm_medium=social** — the type of channel (organic social)
- **utm_campaign=may-content** — a campaign name; "may-content" if this is one of several May posts driving to the same offer
- **utm_content=naming-problems-post** — the specific post, so you can compare individual post performance

This URL is what goes in the post. When someone clicks it, the Attribution Report on the resulting contact shows utm_source=linkedin, utm_campaign=may-content, utm_content=naming-problems-post. Later, when that contact moves through the pipeline to Won, you can pull a report showing exactly how many deals came from this specific post.

A shortened URL via a link shortener (bit.ly, or VibeReach's built-in URL shortener under **Marketing → Trigger Links**) makes the social post look cleaner. The UTM data still passes through to attribution because the shortener redirects to the full UTM-tagged URL behind the scenes. Use VibeReach's Trigger Links specifically — they integrate with Conversations and workflows, so a click on a trigger link in a social post can fire other automation downstream.

## Step 6 — Schedule the Post

Back to the Social Planner composer:

1. Paste the final post content into the caption field.
2. Attach an image (1080x1080 square works well for LinkedIn; 1200x627 horizontal is also fine).
3. Toggle on your LinkedIn personal profile in the account selector. Toggle off any other accounts.
4. Click **Schedule for Later.**
5. Pick a publish date and time. For LinkedIn, the highest-engagement windows are usually Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday between 8-10am and 12-1pm in your audience's primary time zone. Skip weekends and skip Mondays — both perform poorly.
6. Click **Schedule.**

The post appears on the Social Planner calendar at the scheduled time, color-coded by platform. You can drag-and-drop it to a different date/time if your plans change. You can click it to edit the content (until publish time — once it goes live, you can't edit retroactively).

## Step 7 — The Content Calendar View

Switch to **Calendar view** in the Social Planner (a toggle at the top). You now see a monthly view of every scheduled post across every platform, color-coded.

This view is where the system thinking pays off. Look at the calendar:

- Are there gaps? Days or weeks with no posts scheduled? Those are gaps in your top-of-funnel.
- Are posts clustering? Three posts on one day, then nothing for a week? That's bad pacing.
- Is the platform mix right? If LinkedIn is your strongest channel, are most of your scheduled posts going to LinkedIn?

The goal isn't volume. The goal is *predictable presence.* Two thoughtful LinkedIn posts a week, every week, for six months, outperforms ten LinkedIn posts in one week followed by silence.

### Batch Production

The practical workflow that works for solo operators: block 2-3 hours every Sunday afternoon, sit down with your plan-on-paper notes, write 3-5 posts, schedule them all to publish across the coming week. You spend two hours on Sunday and zero hours during the week. The system handles the rest.

## Step 8 — Bulk CSV Import (For Heavier Calendars)

If you want to schedule 30+ posts at once (say, a quarter of content for a client), use the bulk CSV import.

1. In the Social Planner, click **+ New Post → Bulk Upload via CSV.**
2. Download the CSV template.
3. Fill in: post content, image URL, scheduled date/time, target account(s), per row.
4. Upload the completed CSV.
5. The Social Planner validates the file and shows a preview. Confirm and the posts are scheduled in batch.

This is more useful for agencies managing multiple client accounts than for solo operators, but it's there when you need it.

## Step 9 — Recurring and Evergreen Posts

Two specialty post types worth knowing:

**Recurring Posts.** A post that publishes on a repeating schedule — daily, weekly, monthly. Good for standing offers that don't change ("Every Tuesday: free 20-minute CRM audit, book here: [link]"). Configure under **Schedule Options → Create Recurring Post.**

**Evergreen Posts.** A queue of posts the Social Planner cycles through and republishes over time. Good for high-performing posts you want to recirculate quarterly. Create under **+ New Post → Create Evergreen Post.** Add posts to the queue, set a cadence (e.g., one evergreen post per week), the Social Planner automatically picks one and schedules it.

A note: recirculating posts works on platforms like Twitter and (to a lesser extent) Facebook, where feed velocity is high. On LinkedIn, the same post going out repeatedly within a 6-month window can hurt your reach because the algorithm notices duplicates. Use evergreen sparingly on LinkedIn, more freely on faster platforms.

## Step 10 — Measure Results

Two weeks after your post goes live, open the Reporting tools from Tutorial 8 and look at three things:

**In Social Planner → Statistics.** Reach, impressions, clicks, engagement (likes/comments/shares). This is platform-level data on the post itself. Was it a hit on LinkedIn? Did it reach 500 people or 5,000?

**In Reporting → Attribution → Source Reports.** Filter on utm_source=linkedin, utm_campaign=may-content. See how many contacts came from LinkedIn over the period. Did the post drive traffic that actually became contacts?

**In Reporting → Attribution → drill into specific contacts from this source.** Did any of those LinkedIn-sourced contacts make it to Call Booked? To Proposal Sent? To Won? If yes, what was the deal value? That's your actual ROI from the post.

Most posts won't produce closed deals on their own. The point of consistent posting is that the cumulative effect, across dozens of posts over months, is what drives revenue — not any single post. But the per-post measurement tells you which *kinds* of posts produce results. After 20 posts and the attribution data behind each, patterns emerge. Storytelling posts outperform list posts. Posts with specific dollar figures outperform vague posts. Posts that mention a specific industry outperform broad ones. You learn what your audience responds to. You make more of that. The system compounds.
