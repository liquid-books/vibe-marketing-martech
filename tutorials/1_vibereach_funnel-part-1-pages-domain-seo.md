# Building Your First Funnel in VibeReach — From Template to Launch

*A step-by-step tutorial for the absolute beginner. We will create a real, working funnel using the CRM template called "Acquire Customers on Autopilot," edit both pages, attach a custom domain on a fresh subdomain, walk through every option in the editor, optimize for SEO and AI search, and finish by setting up an A/B test.*

---

## Why You Are About to Spend an Hour Doing This

Before we touch a single button, let me tell you what a funnel really is and why this matters more than building a "website."

A funnel is a sequence of pages designed to do one thing — move a stranger from "I just saw this" to "I just paid you" (or "I just booked you," or "I just gave you my number"). A website is a brochure. A funnel is a conveyor belt. Every page on a funnel has exactly one job and exactly one next step. When you build a funnel correctly, you stop chasing leads and the leads start chasing you, which is the whole reason VibeReach built a template called *Acquire Customers on Autopilot* in the first place.

The work you are about to do has three layers, and each one matters:

1. **The mechanical layer** — getting the pages live on your own domain with the right plumbing.
2. **The copy layer** — what the words on the page actually say. *This is where 80% of the result comes from.* A beautiful funnel with weak copy converts at 1%. An ugly funnel with sharp copy converts at 10%. You can have the wrong colors and the wrong font and still win, but you cannot have the wrong words.
3. **The discoverability layer** — SEO, AI search optimization, and tracking. This is the part most people skip, and it's the part that decides whether your funnel is found by anyone at all.

We are going to cover all three. Let's go.

## Step 1 — Navigate to the Funnel Builder

From your sub-account dashboard, go to **Sites > Funnels** in the left-hand navigation. This is the home base for every funnel in this sub-account. You'll see a list of any existing funnels and, in the top right, a **New Funnel** button.

Click **New Funnel**. VibeReach will present three options:

- **From Blank** — you start with an empty canvas and build every section yourself.
- **Funnel AI** — you describe what you want in plain language and the AI generates a page (the first five generations are free per sub-account; additional builds cost $0.99 each).
- **From Templates** — you pick a proven, pre-built funnel designed by VibeReach's team or community.

We are using **From Templates**. Click it, then click **Continue**.

**Why templates win for your first funnel:** A template gives you a professional design that has already been proven to convert. You aren't fighting blank-canvas paralysis. You're fine-tuning copy and brand against a structure that already works. Save your "build from scratch" energy for funnel #20, after you understand what a good one feels like.

## Step 3 — Edit Page One: The Landing Page (The Capture)

Click the first step. The VibeReach page editor opens. Take a breath — this looks like a lot, but the structure is simple once you see it.

The page is built out of a hierarchy: **Section > Row > Column > Element.** A section spans the full width of the page (your "hero," your "testimonials," your "footer" — those are sections). Inside a section you place rows. Inside rows you place columns. Inside columns you place elements (a headline, a button, an image, a form). Every element you see on the page lives inside that nested structure. Once that clicks, the whole builder becomes obvious.

### Editing Copy on This Page

Click any text on the page. The block editor opens and you can type directly. Replace every word of placeholder copy with your own business copy. **This is the most important moment in this entire tutorial.** Here is the framework I want you to use for the landing page:

- **Headline (the H1):** State the specific outcome you deliver and who it's for. Generic = "Grow Your Business." Specific = "Auto Repair Shops in South Florida: Fill Your Bays with Local Customers in 30 Days." The specific version wins every time because the visitor instantly knows, "this is for me."
- **Sub-headline:** State the mechanism — *how* you deliver that outcome. "Without spending more on ads. Without hiring a marketing agency. Using a single CRM automation we'll set up for you in a free 20-minute call."
- **Three to five bullet outcomes:** Each one names a specific result, not a feature. Not "AI-powered workflows." Instead, "Every missed call gets a text-back in 90 seconds, so the lead never reaches your competitor."
- **The single call to action:** One button. One destination. "Book My Free Setup Call" — not "Learn More," not "Click Here." The button text should describe what the visitor gets when they click.
- **Social proof:** Logos, a testimonial, a number ("113,000 students," "$5.2B in sales facilitated"). Visitors need permission to believe you. Social proof is that permission.

The reason copy matters more than design: every visitor is silently asking three questions in their first eight seconds — *Is this for me? Can I trust them? What do I do next?* If your copy answers those three questions immediately, you have already beaten 95% of the funnels on the internet.

### Adding and Rearranging Components: The Plus Sign

You will see a **plus sign (+)** appear whenever you hover between sections, rows, or columns. That plus sign is how you add new building blocks. Click it and the element drawer opens. You can search by keyword (try "headline," "button," "form," "video," "calendar," "countdown") and VibeReach will surface the matching elements even if you don't remember the exact name. Drag and drop the element where you want it.

Common elements you'll use on a landing page:

- **Heading** — your H1 and H2 text blocks
- **Paragraph / Rich Text** — your body copy
- **Button** — your call-to-action
- **Image** — your hero image, screenshots, logos
- **Form** — to capture name, email, phone
- **Calendar** — to let visitors book directly from the page
- **Video** — embed a short pitch (under 90 seconds is the sweet spot)
- **Countdown Timer** — useful for time-limited offers
- **Custom Code** — for embedding outside widgets

Every element you click reveals a right-hand sidebar with three tabs: **General** (the content — the text in the headline, the URL on the button), **Styles** (the look — margin, padding, border, alignment, mobile/tablet visibility), and **Animations** (motion on scroll or hover). Most beginners only need General and Styles. Animations are powerful but they're a distraction until your copy is locked.

## Step 4 — The Top Toolbar: Every Option Explained

Across the top of the editor, you have a row of icons. Each one opens a different aspect of the page. Here's what every single one does and why it matters.

### The Plus Sign (Add Elements)

Already covered above — this is your element library. Open it any time you need to add a new piece to the page. Use the search box; it's faster than scrolling.

### Layers

Layers is a tree view of every section, row, column, and element on the page. Think of it like the Photoshop layers panel. When your page gets complex — multiple overlapping sections, sticky headers, popups — Layers becomes essential because you can select an element that's hard to click on the canvas, drag it to reorder, rename it for your own clarity ("Hero Section" instead of "Section 1"), or temporarily hide it. **The value of Layers is structural visibility.** Once your page has 30+ elements, clicking around the canvas is slow. Working from the Layers panel is fast.

### Pages

This shows every page (every "step") inside this funnel. Right now we have two — the landing page and the thank-you page. You can switch between them instantly from this menu, which beats backing out to the funnel steps view every time. You can also add new steps from here. As your funnel matures, you'll add a sales page, an order bump, a one-click upsell, a thank-you with a calendar embed, etc.

### Tracking Code

This is where you paste scripts that need to fire on the page — Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, Meta Pixel, TikTok Pixel, Hotjar heatmaps, custom JavaScript. You have a **Head** section (loads before the page renders) and a **Body** section (loads inside the body or just before the closing tag). **Why this matters:** without tracking code, you have no idea where your traffic is coming from, what people are doing on your page, or which ads are actually working. Tracking code is the difference between marketing and guessing.

A practical rule: install Google Tag Manager in the Head, and then manage all your other pixels and scripts from inside GTM. That way you can add or remove tracking later without ever touching VibeReach again.

### Custom CSS

This is the override panel for visual styling. If something looks slightly off — a button that needs a tighter corner radius, a section that needs more breathing room on mobile, a font you want to weight differently — you write the CSS rule here. You don't have to know CSS to use VibeReach, but knowing even a tiny bit unlocks tremendous polish. If you don't write code, you can ignore this tab and the builder will still produce a clean, professional page. The Custom CSS tab is the escape hatch when the default controls don't go far enough.

### Typography

This is your global font and color control for the whole funnel. You choose your **Headline Font** (used for H1, H2, H3 etc.) and your **Content Font** (used for paragraph text). You also set default text color and link color. **Why this matters more than people think:** consistent typography is the single fastest way to make a funnel feel "designed" rather than "thrown together." Pick one headline font (something with personality — a serif or bold sans), one content font (something boring and readable — Inter, Open Sans, Helvetica), and apply them across every page. Don't use more than two fonts in the entire funnel.

VibeReach now supports uploading your own custom font files (.ttf, .otf, .woff, .woff2) directly in the builder, so brand fidelity is no longer a fight with CSS.

### Background

This sets the default background for the whole page — a solid color, a gradient, or a background image. Most landing pages should use a solid light background (white or very pale gray) for the main content sections, with darker sections used sparingly for emphasis (testimonials, the footer, a high-contrast CTA section). A busy background fights with your copy. Your background should disappear, not perform.

### Popup Settings

A popup is a modal that appears over the page — exit-intent offers, timed offers, lead-capture forms triggered by a click. The popup settings panel lets you control when popups appear (on page load, after X seconds, on exit intent, when a specific element is clicked) and what they contain. **Use them sparingly.** A well-timed exit-intent popup that offers a discount can rescue 5-10% of bouncing visitors. A popup that fires the second someone lands on your page makes them close the tab. Test conservatively.

### SEO Meta Data and the AI Search Preview

I want you to spend more time on this tab than any other. This is where you make your funnel **findable**. Skipping this step is the single most common mistake I see in VibeReach sites — pages go live with titles like "Step 1" and no meta description at all.

I'm going to break this section out in detail in Step 6 because it deserves its own moment.

### Cookie Consent

This is your GDPR / CCPA / privacy-law compliance banner. Click the cookie icon to enable the banner, then choose your settings.

The VibeReach cookie banner supports two main modes:

- **Ask to Opt-In** — shows "Accept Essential" and "Accept All" buttons (this is what you want for EU and UK visitors).
- **Don't Ask** — shows only an "OK" button (a notification, not a consent gate).

You can also configure **cookie categories** (Essential, Functional, Analytics, Performance, Advertising), set **consent expiration** (how many days before the banner re-asks), choose a **layout style** (bar at bottom, modal in corner, full overlay), and target the banner by **geography** (Worldwide, EU & UK only, or specific countries you select).

**Why this matters legally:** if you have any visitors from the EU, UK, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, or Virginia — which you do, even if you don't think you do — you have legal exposure if you collect data without consent. The VibeReach banner is a presentation layer for compliance; you still need an actual privacy policy page and you should have a lawyer review it. But it's a real step, and it's free, and you should turn it on today.

**Why this matters for trust:** visitors who see a thoughtful cookie banner trust the site more. It signals professionalism. The funnel pages that omit it look like they were thrown together yesterday.

### Save, Preview, Publish

Top right corner — always save before you leave the editor. Use Preview to see exactly what the visitor sees, including how the tracking code and custom code behave (they're injected client-side so they only show in the rendered preview, not in the page source).

## Step 5 — Edit Page Two: The Thank-You Page

Click back to **Pages** and switch to the second step in your funnel. This is the thank-you / next-step page — what your visitor sees the moment they submit the form.

Most people waste this page. They write "Thanks for signing up!" and leave it at that. That is a six-figure mistake. The thank-you page is the moment of maximum attention you will ever have from this person. They just took an action. They are leaning in. They are paying attention. You have one job here: **tell them what happens next.**

A strong thank-you page does some or all of these things:

- Confirms what they just did and what to expect ("Your guide is on its way to your inbox in the next 60 seconds. It'll come from Dr. Ernesto Lee — please mark it as 'not spam' so you don't miss it.")
- Adds a calendar embed so they can book the next step right now while they're hot
- Includes a short personal video from you (recorded on your phone, 60 seconds, just say "thanks, here's what's coming, here's the one thing you should do today")
- Offers a tripwire — a small, irresistible paid offer ($7, $17, $27) that converts the lead into a buyer instantly
- Includes a "share this with a friend" link with pre-filled SMS or email copy

Repeat the same copy and design discipline from page one. Same fonts. Same colors. Same voice. The thank-you page is part of the same conversation, not a different one.

## Step 6 — SEO and AI Search Optimization (The Long Conversation)

This is the part of the tutorial that will pay for itself a hundred times over. I'm going to slow down here because most VibeReach users never do this work, and the ones who do beat the ones who don't.

### What Has Changed About Search Since 2024

For twenty-five years, "SEO" meant one thing: get your page to rank on Google's results pages. That world still exists, but a second world has appeared next to it. Today, roughly **700 million people per week** use ChatGPT alone. Google itself now answers a huge percentage of queries directly with **AI Overviews** — an AI-generated summary that appears above the traditional ten blue links. Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and other AI tools do the same thing.

What this means in practice: when a potential customer of yours types "best CRM consultant in South Florida" into ChatGPT, the AI doesn't show them ten links. It reads a handful of pages it considers authoritative and writes one paragraph of recommendations. **You either appear in that paragraph or you don't exist.** There is no scroll. There is no page two.

The pages that AI tools cite and pull from are the ones with **clean structure and structured data** — clear headings in a logical hierarchy, descriptive meta titles, accurate meta descriptions, alt text on images, canonical URLs, and schema markup. Pages that are sloppy or under-labeled get skipped, even if their content is excellent.

This is why the SEO Meta Data tab inside your VibeReach funnel editor is one of the most important features in the platform. Let's go through it.

### How to Fill Out the SEO Meta Data Tab

Inside the funnel editor, click the **Settings** icon in the top bar, then select **SEO Meta Data.** You'll see fields for:

**Meta Title (Page Title).** This is the headline that appears in Google search results and in the browser tab. Keep it under 60–70 characters. Front-load your primary keyword. "Acquire Customers on Autopilot" is the template's marketing name — your actual meta title should describe what *your* business does. For example: *"South Florida CRM Setup for Service Businesses — Dr. Ernesto Lee."* Notice the structure: keyword phrase, who it's for, branded name. Google rewards specificity.

**Meta Description.** This is the two-line summary under your title in search results. Keep it under 155–160 characters. Write it like ad copy, because that's what it is — its only job is to earn the click. Don't describe the page; pitch the page. *"Stop losing leads to missed calls and slow follow-up. We set up the CRM automation that books appointments while you sleep. Free 20-minute setup call."*

**URL Slug (Step Path).** The end of the URL — `yourdomain.com/setup-call` is better than `yourdomain.com/page-1-step-1-v2-final-FINAL`. Use short, keyword-rich, lowercase, hyphen-separated slugs. Once a page is live, don't change the slug — every existing link to it will break.

**Favicon.** The little icon that appears in browser tabs. Upload your logo cropped to a square. This is one of those tiny details that signals professionalism. Funnels without a favicon look unfinished.

**Image (Open Graph / Social Preview).** This is the image that shows up when someone shares your page on Facebook, LinkedIn, iMessage, Slack, or anywhere else. Upload a 1200×630 pixel image that includes your headline and brand. **VibeReach ships this field empty by default**, which means every link to your page on social platforms looks broken. Fix this on every page.

**Social Preview Title and Description.** Same idea — the title and description that appear in the social preview card. Don't reuse your meta title verbatim. The social audience is different from the search audience. Write something more emotional, more curiosity-driven, more "you have to click this."

**Custom Meta Tags (under Links and Tags).** This is where you can add specific instructions for search engines and AI crawlers. The most important one to know: a `robots` tag with content `noindex` tells search engines *not* to index that specific page. **You will want to noindex every step in your funnel except your main landing page.** Why? Because if you let Google index your thank-you page, your order page, and your upsell page, you create duplicate-content competition with your real landing page, and Google gets confused about which one to surface. The VibeReach community has documented that 7 out of 10 audited VibeReach accounts have this exact problem unfixed.

**Schema Markup.** Under the same Links and Tags area you can paste JSON-LD structured data — the technical signals that tell AI systems and search engines exactly what your page is *about* in a machine-readable format. For a service business landing page, add a `LocalBusiness` schema with your name, address, phone, opening hours, and services. This is the single biggest lever for showing up in Google's local pack and in AI search results that ask "who is the best X near me?"

### What "AI Search Preview" Means Inside VibeReach

VibeReach's Funnels & Websites AI feature (newer, currently in beta) auto-generates meta titles, meta descriptions, and image alt text with keyword-rich suggestions when it creates a page. You can also use the AI panel inside the editor to ask it to rewrite your meta data based on the page content. **Whether you use the AI panel or write it yourself, the discipline is the same: every page gets a title, every page gets a description, every page gets a social preview image, every page gets the right indexing instructions.** No exceptions.

The bigger point: AI search readiness is not a feature you buy. It's a habit you build. Five minutes of meta data work per page is the difference between a funnel that quietly compounds organic visibility for years, and a funnel that requires paid ads forever.

## Step 7 — Attach a Custom Domain on a New Subdomain

Right now, your funnel lives at a URL that looks something like `something.gohighlevel-pages.com`. That's fine for testing. It is not okay for sharing. A custom domain — `yourbrand.com` or `funnels.yourbrand.com` — signals legitimacy, supports your SEO, and lets people actually remember the link.

### Why a Subdomain, Not Your Root Domain

You probably already have a website living at `yourbrand.com`. Don't replace it. Instead, point a **subdomain** to your VibeReach funnel — something like `book.yourbrand.com`, `offers.yourbrand.com`, or `go.yourbrand.com`. This keeps your main site untouched and gives your funnels their own clean address that you can change or take down without affecting anything else. Agencies and serious operators almost always run funnels on subdomains.

### Creating the Subdomain

Step one happens at your domain registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare, Google Domains, etc. — wherever you bought the domain). Step two happens inside VibeReach. They have to agree, or the connection fails.

**At your domain registrar:**

1. Log in. Go to the DNS management area for your domain.
2. Add a new **CNAME record.**
3. For the **Name** or **Host** field, type the subdomain you want — for example, `book`. (Don't type the full `book.yourbrand.com`; just the part before your root domain.)
4. For the **Value** or **Target** field, enter VibeReach's funnel hosting target. The most commonly used target is `funnels.msgsndr.com`, but **the exact value VibeReach gives you on the connect screen is the authoritative one — copy it from there, not from a blog post.**
5. Set **TTL** to 1 hour (or your provider's default).
6. **Critical for Cloudflare users:** set the proxy status to **DNS Only** (gray cloud), not Proxied (orange cloud). VibeReach does not support Cloudflare's proxy.
7. Save.

**Inside VibeReach:**

1. Go to **Settings > Domains.**
2. Click **Add Domain** (or **Connect a Domain**).
3. Enter the full subdomain — `book.yourbrand.com`.
4. Click **Continue.** VibeReach will display the exact DNS record values you need (in case you didn't grab them earlier).
5. Once your DNS records are saved at the registrar, click **Verify Records.** VibeReach checks each record and shows a green checkmark when it sees them.
6. Select the funnel you want to attach to this domain, and choose which step should be the default landing page (Step 1 of your funnel).
7. Click **Link Domain** (or **Proceed to Finish**).

DNS propagation usually takes 15 minutes to an hour but can take up to 24 hours in rare cases. VibeReach will automatically issue and install an SSL certificate (HTTPS) for your domain as soon as it verifies the records. You don't need to buy or upload a cert separately — that's handled.

Once linked, your funnel is live at `https://book.yourbrand.com`. Test it. Share it. Make sure the form submits, the thank-you page loads, and the confirmation email arrives.

## Step 8 — Set Up an A/B Split Test

Now you're going to test whether your headline (or your button, or your hero image) is actually the best version. This is the discipline that separates marketers who guess from marketers who know.

### What an A/B Test Actually Is

You create a second version of your page — called the **Variation** — that differs from the original (the **Control**) by exactly one thing. You let VibeReach send half your traffic to each version. After enough visitors come through, the version with the higher conversion rate wins, and that's the version you keep. Then you create the next variation and test the next thing. Over months, you compound improvements that would be impossible to achieve through guessing alone.

A real example: a language-learning company tested *"Learn Spanish in Just 30 Days"* against *"Unlock the Secrets to Mastering Spanish Fast."* One of those will outperform the other by a meaningful margin. The point isn't which one — the point is that without testing, you'd never know.

### Creating the Variation in VibeReach

1. Go to **Sites > Funnels** and open your funnel.
2. Click the step (page) you want to split test. This page becomes your **Control.**
3. Click **Create Variation.** VibeReach offers two options:
   - **Duplicate the control page** (faster — clones the current page and lets you edit it).
   - **Create from blank** (start fresh).
4. For most tests, duplicate. Then change exactly **one thing** in the variation — the headline, the button text, the hero image, the form length. *Only one.* If you change three things at once, you won't know which one moved the result.
5. Use the **traffic split slider** to decide what percentage of visitors see each version. Start at 50/50 unless you have a reason to be more cautious.
6. Click **Start Split Test.**

### Reading the Results

VibeReach shows live stats inside the funnel editor — page views, opt-ins, and conversion rate for both Control and Variation. Wait until you have **enough traffic for the result to be statistically meaningful.** A 2% lead over 50 visitors is noise. A 2% lead over 2,000 visitors is signal. As a beginner's rule, don't declare a winner under 200 conversions per variation, and target 95% statistical confidence (you can use a free split-test calculator online to check).

Once you have a clear winner, hover over the winning page and click **Declare this as Winner.** All future traffic flows to that page. Then start your next test.

### What to Test First

Test in this priority order — biggest leverage first:

1. **Headline.** Always test the headline first. It's the single highest-leverage element on the page.
2. **Hero image or video.** What's at the top above the fold has more influence than anything else below it.
3. **Call-to-action button text.** "Book My Free Setup Call" vs "Get Started" vs "Yes, I Want This."
4. **Form length.** Three fields vs five fields. Shorter usually wins, but not always.
5. **Social proof placement.** Above the fold vs below the fold.

A few rules that will save you from misleading yourself:

- Only run **one test at a time** on a given page.
- Once a test is running, **do not edit the control.** Let both versions sit unchanged while the data accumulates.
- Don't peek and quit. Set the test, set a calendar reminder, come back in two weeks.
- Be aware that seasonality, ad campaign changes, and day-of-week effects can skew short-running tests. Long enough = more reliable.

## Step 9 — Final Checklist Before You Send Traffic

Before you spend a single dollar on ads or a single hour on outreach, walk this list:

- Both pages have unique, keyword-rich meta titles and meta descriptions.
- Every step except the main landing page is set to `noindex`.
- Open Graph image is uploaded on every page (1200×630).
- Favicon is uploaded.
- Cookie consent banner is enabled with Ask to Opt-In mode.
- Custom domain (subdomain) is verified and SSL is active.
- The form on page one is connected to a CRM workflow that fires an instant confirmation email and SMS.
- Tracking code is installed (Google Tag Manager in the head; Meta Pixel, GA4, and any ad pixels live inside GTM).
- The page loads in under 3 seconds on mobile (test it; if not, compress images and enable VibeReach's image optimization).
- You've previewed every page on mobile *and* desktop. Most traffic is mobile. If mobile is broken, you're broken.
- One A/B test is running on the highest-leverage element (start with the headline).

When all of those are green, you launch.

Templates handle the structure. SEO and AI optimization handle the discovery. Domain and SSL handle the trust. A/B testing handles the improvement. But it's the words — yours, written for a real human you've actually spoken to — that handle the conversion.

Go write them.
